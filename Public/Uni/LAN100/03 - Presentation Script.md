# Presentation Script: My Future Goals

A model script for the LAN 100 oral presentation, built from your own slide points. It follows the structure in [[Public/Uni/LAN100/01 - Oral Presentation Guide|Oral Presentation Guide]] and goes with [[Public/Uni/LAN100/02 - My Future Goals.pptx|My Future Goals.pptx]].

> [!warning] Use it to practise, not to read
> The rubric rewards **explaining, not reading** (*Delivery & Communication* and *Spoken Language*). Memorising this word for word sounds read even if you never look at it. Read the script a few times to find good phrases, then rehearse **from the cue cards** (below) and let the wording change each time.

---

## Timing at a Glance

At a normal pace (~130 words per minute), the script runs about **4½ minutes**, inside the 4–5 minute window.

| Part | Slides | Words | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Introduction | 1–2 | ~65 | ~30 s |
| Academic goal | 3 | ~125 | ~55 s |
| Career goal | 4 | ~130 | ~60 s |
| Web app goal | 5 | ~125 | ~55 s |
| Free Choice: China | 6 | ~110 | ~50 s |
| Conclusion | 7 | ~50 | ~25 s |
| **Total** | | **~605** | **~4:35** |

> [!tip] Running long or short?
> - **Over 5:00:** cut the second sentence of each "why" explanation. Never cut the researched fact.
> - **Under 4:00:** you're probably rushing. Pause after each goal, and add the optional example sentences marked *(optional)*.

## The Script

### Introduction (slides 1–2)

Good morning, everyone. My name is George Demetriou, and today I'm going to talk about my future goals. I'll present four of them: an academic goal, a career goal, a personal project I want to build, and a country I'd love to visit. For each one, I'll explain why it matters to me, how I plan to achieve it, and one fact I found while researching it.

### Goal 1: Academic (slide 3)

Let's start with my academic goal. I want to graduate with a GPA of 8.5 or higher, out of 10.

Why does this matter to me? First, a high GPA could open doors to a master's degree later on. Second, aiming high pushes me to really understand my subjects instead of just passing them, so it deepens my knowledge of my field.

So how am I going to achieve it? Through consistent studying and early planning. Instead of leaving everything until the week before the exams, I want to study a little every week and start my assignments early.

While I was researching this, I found something interesting on the University of Edinburgh's website. For master's programmes that require a first-class degree, they ask students from Cyprus for about 8.5 out of 10. So 8.5 isn't a random number: it's the level that keeps the best options open.

### Goal 2: Career (slide 4)

Moving on to my career goal. After I graduate, I'd like to work as a software engineer at a tech company here in Cyprus.

The main reason is that I genuinely enjoy dealing with complex problems. When something doesn't work, I want to find out why, and solving it is the most satisfying part of programming for me. The second reason is simple: I don't plan to leave Cyprus in the near future, so I want to build my career here.

To reach this goal, I'm going to try to get an internship before I graduate, so that I already have some real work experience. I also want to build a decent portfolio of projects to show employers what I can do.

And the good news is that the industry here is growing. According to Eurostat, the number of ICT specialists in Cyprus grew by almost 10% a year between 2015 and 2025. That was the second-fastest growth in the whole EU, so I'm optimistic that there will be opportunities for me.

### Goal 3: Hobby / Interest, Web App (slide 5)

My third goal is connected to the previous one. In my free time, I want to build a web application that solves a real problem. *(optional: For example, a simple tool that helps students keep track of their deadlines.)*

This matters to me for two reasons. First, it would be a real project to show employers, which says much more than a list of courses on a CV. Second, I'll learn a lot while I'm building it, because a real project always teaches you things that a course doesn't.

My plan is to learn the necessary tools first, and then start small and expand later. I'd rather finish a simple version that works than plan something huge and never finish it.

One of those tools will definitely be JavaScript. According to the 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey, it's the most used programming language: 66% of developers said they had used it in the past year.

### Goal 4: Free Choice, Travel to China (slide 6)

Finally, my free choice goal is a personal one: I'd love to travel to China, probably after I graduate.

I'm interested in China because its culture is so different from ours, and because it has thousands of years of history that I'd like to see with my own eyes, not just on a screen.

To make it happen, I'm going to save a small amount of money every month, and I'd like to learn a few basic phrases in Mandarin before I go.

The first place on my list is the Great Wall. According to UNESCO, it was built over almost two thousand years, from the third century BC to the seventeenth century, and its total length is more than 20,000 kilometres.

> [!question] ⚠️ Check: your reasons and plan for China
> You didn't give me "why" or "how" points for China, so the ones above (**culture, history** / **save monthly, basic Mandarin**) are my suggestions. Change them to your real reasons. Your own reasons are easier to explain and sound more natural.

### Conclusion (slide 7)

To sum up, my four goals are connected. A good GPA and my own projects should help me get a job as a software engineer here in Cyprus, and the trip to China will be my reward at the end of it all. Thank you for listening. Are there any questions?

## Language Used (B2 points)

The script deliberately mixes structures, which helps the *Spoken Language* mark:

| Structure | Where in the script |
|---|---|
| **be going to** (plans) | "I'm **going to** try to get an internship" |
| **want / would like / would love to** | "I'**d like to** work as…", "I'**d love to** travel to China" |
| **would rather … than** | "I'**d rather** finish a simple version **than** plan something huge" |
| **could** (possibility) | "a high GPA **could** open doors" |
| **so that** (purpose) | "**so that** I already have some real work experience" |
| **Present perfect / past** for research | "**I found** something interesting", "66% **had used** it" |
| **Reporting a source** | "**According to** Eurostat…", "they **ask** students from Cyprus for…" |
| **Signposting** | "Let's start with…", "Moving on to…", "My third goal is connected to…", "Finally,…", "To sum up,…" |

## Possible Questions (and how to answer)

The rubric says the grade comes from *"what the student can explain live in class"*. Be ready for these:

> [!example]- "Is 8.5 realistic for you?"
> *"It's ambitious, but I think it's realistic if I'm consistent from the first year. Even if I end up a little below it, aiming high will still help me."*

> [!example]- "Why not work abroad, where salaries are higher?"
> *"Salaries can be higher abroad, but living costs are higher too, and my family is here. Also, the tech sector in Cyprus is growing quickly, as the Eurostat data shows, so I don't feel I have to leave."*

> [!example]- "What problem will your web app solve?"
> Have one concrete answer ready, e.g. *"A tool that helps students track deadlines from all their courses in one place."* A vague answer here weakens the whole goal.

> [!example]- "Where exactly did you find the Edinburgh number?"
> *"On the University of Edinburgh's admissions page for students from Cyprus. They compare the Cypriot 10-point scale to UK degree classes: 8.5 for a first-class degree and 7.5 for a 2:1."*

> [!example]- "Why is JavaScript important for your goal?"
> *"JavaScript runs in every web browser, so it's the main language for the interactive part of a web app. That's why so many developers use it."*

> [!example]- "Why the Great Wall first?"
> *"It's the most famous symbol of Chinese history, and it was built over almost two thousand years, so it connects to what interests me most about China."*

## Common Mistakes When Using a Script

> [!warning] Common mistakes
> - **Memorising it word for word.** One forgotten word and the whole section collapses. Remember the **ideas** (cue cards), not the sentences.
> - **Reading the source sentence aloud.** Always paraphrase: *"According to Eurostat, …"* plus your own words.
> - **Saying the citation brackets aloud** ("open bracket, Eurostat, 2026"). Just name the source naturally; the slide shows the citation.
> - **Rushing the numbers.** Slow down on **8.5**, **10%**, **66%** and **20,000 km**: they're your evidence.
> - **No pause between goals.** A one-second pause plus "Moving on to…" tells the audience a new section has started.
> - **Rehearsing silently.** Only time yourself **out loud, standing up**.

In-text citations on the slides: (University of Edinburgh, n.d.) · (Eurostat, 2026) · (Stack Overflow, 2025) · (UNESCO World Heritage Centre, n.d.)

> [!note] Why not a visa fact for China?
> Cypriot citizens can currently enter China visa-free for up to 30 days, but that policy is only confirmed **until 31 December 2026**, before you'd travel. A fact that may no longer be true by your trip makes a weak "researched fact", so the script uses the Great Wall instead.
